A practical toolkit for partners delivering focused Shopify builds — typically on Shopify Plus, sometimes Advanced/Grow — for a single merchant or brand. It defines what “good” looks like across your core deliverables in Shopify’s standards. Use it to set projects up to succeed and to protect your scope. Partners may use their own formats; these templates describe the quality bar, not a required layout.

Plus capabilities to scope and leverage where they fit: Checkout Extensibility, Shopify Functions, B2B, Shopify Markets and expansion stores, Shopify Flow, POS Pro, and organization-level user permissions.

How to right-size as you read: keep every section, but match its depth to the project. A theme-and-apps build needs a light touch; a Plus B2B build with a couple of integrations needs more. The goal is clarity, not paperwork.

1. Statement of Work (SoW) Quality Template

Statement of Work templateA fill-in version of Section 1 with the headings, tables and prompts ready to complete. Adapt it to your own format.
Download SoW template (.docx)

Purpose

The SoW should make crystal clear what you’re responsible for delivering, what’s excluded, when key deliverables land, what your scope assumes, and who owns what. On a focused build, scope clarity and explicit exclusions are your main protection against unpaid scope creep — treat them as the most important part of the document.

1.1 Engagement overview and explicit exclusions

AreaWhat to define
Engagement overviewBuild type: net-new build, replatform/migration, theme refresh, B2B/wholesale, POS rollout, or a focused international setup.
Shopify plan assumptionThe plan the scope assumes (Plus by default; note Advanced/Grow if that’s the case) and any plan-dependent capabilities (Checkout Extensibility, Functions, B2B, multiple markets).
Explicit exclusionsWhat’s not included: content creation, data cleanup, SEO audits (validation of SEO/analytics at launch is included; a full SEO audit is not), penetration/load testing, third-party app licensing, ongoing support or retainer work unless stated; ongoing Shop channel optimization (Shop Campaigns, subscriptions, reviews, Sign in with Shop, etc.) beyond initial Shop Store setup
Accessibility and complianceThe build targets [WCAG 2.2 AA] as its conformance standard; the merchant owns ongoing content accessibility after launch. A formal accessibility audit or legal compliance certification is out of scope unless explicitly added. For merchants selling into the EU, treat accessibility as a compliance requirement under the European Accessibility Act, not a best-effort intent.
Success criteriaThe measurable outcomes that define a successful launch: performance targets (e.g. a storefront speed/Core Web Vitals baseline), the key conversion or operational metrics the build is meant to support, and the "definition of done" for go-live. Keep it to a handful of criteria the merchant agrees to up front. Shop Store "complete" (branded, merchandised, with products and active discounts published to Shop; Shop_cash mapped as payment method).
Commercial boundariesWhat’s covered by the fee versus what triggers a change request.

Quality bar: a reader should immediately see what’s in, what’s out, and where a change request begins.

1.2 Scope and requirements

A structured scope section that defines the build. Match depth to the project, and mark anything aspirational as “to confirm in discovery.”

Scope areaWhat to define
StorefrontArchitecture: Theme-based (Online Store 2.0 — which theme/base, customization extent, app embeds) or headless/custom (Hydrogen + Oxygen, or a custom front end on the Storefront API — framework, hosting, and who owns the codebase). Plus performance expectations, accessibility and compliance approach (target standard, e.g. WCAG 2.2 AA, and who owns conformance), and supported browsers/devices.
Product catalogProduct/variant model, collections, metafields/metaobjects, bundles if any, content ownership, search/filtering, source of truth.
Cart and checkoutCheckout customization via Checkout Extensibility, Shopify Functions where relevant, payments, Shop Pay, taxes, shipping, discounts/promotions, and known checkout limits. Checkout customization is via Checkout Extensibility only (checkout.liquid is retired); any legacy Shopify Scripts must be rebuilt as Shopify Functions.
Customer accountsAccount model (new customer accounts), login, profile data, customer migration if any, basic identity needs.
Content and merchandisingKey page types, who builds content, campaign/merchandising needs, localization if relevant, admin manageability.
Sales channelsThe auto-installed Shop channel at minimum: confirm the Shop Store is branded (logo/header) and merchandised (featured collections, custom navigation), with active products and code discounts published to Shop, and note who owns setup. Ongoing Shop optimization (Campaigns, subscriptions, reviews, Sign in with Shop, Follow, post-purchase, Shop Promise) is post-launch/change-request work, not Day-1 scope unless stated.
Order operationsOrder lifecycle, fulfillment, returns/exchanges, notifications, and any operational reporting the merchant needs. Plus any merchant-facing automation (e.g. Shopify Flow for order tagging, fraud holds, or fulfillment routing).
SEO and analyticsURL/redirect strategy (critical for migrations), metadata, analytics and pixels, consent, launch validation.
Apps and integrationsApps/ISVs in scope, what each is for, who owns configuration, cost assumptions, and any limits.
Custom developmentCustom code scope, owner, commercial treatment, and API dependencies.
Volume assumptionsThe counts that drive effort: SKUs, variants, collections, redirects, customers, historical orders, languages, currencies/markets, integrations, and (if relevant) B2B catalogs/price lists, POS locations.
If applicable (keep light)B2B/wholesale (Plus): company accounts, catalogs, price lists, payment terms, draft orders/quotes (skip credit limits, account hierarchies, and ERP account sync unless truly in scope). POS: locations in scope, hardware ownership, inventory sync, BOPIS if any, cross-channel returns. International: Shopify Markets — countries, currencies, languages, domains, and per-market catalog/pricing differences (skip landed-cost/duties programs unless in scope).

Quality bar: it should be clear where the solution is Shopify-native, where it leans on an app, and where it’s custom — and how each is treated commercially.

1.4 Integrations and data migration scope

Define integration and migration obligations at the depth the project actually needs.

ElementWhat to define
Systems in scopeThe apps/integrations genuinely present (e.g., an ERP or 3PL connector, tax, reviews, email, analytics; middleware only if truly used).
System of recordFor products, inventory, pricing, customers, orders.
OwnershipWho builds and owns each integration (partner, merchant, app vendor).
Data flowsDirection, sync timing/frequency, and basic error-handling assumptions.
Migration scopeObjects to migrate (products, variants, collections, customers, orders, redirects, content, metafields), plus historical data if in scope.
ValidationHow migrated data is checked (a structured spot-check) and the access needed to do it.

Quality bar: specific enough to surface delivery risk before build starts — without inventing enterprise systems-integration-testing machinery the project doesn’t have.

1.6 Ownership, dependencies, assumptions, and commercial controls

AreaWhat to define
Partner-ownedDeliverables, project management, build, app configuration, custom development, integration work, testing support, launch support, documentation, post-launch support.
Merchant-ownedBusiness decisions, stakeholder availability, content, product data, data cleanup, system access, third-party contracts, approvals, test execution, launch sign-off.
Shopify dependency assumptions (if any)Any expected Shopify advisory, launch guidance, or Plus enablement, stated as assumptions (Shopify isn’t a signatory to the SoW).
Third-party dependency assumptionsApp/middleware setup, payment/tax provider setup, data access, expected timelines.
CommunicationSingle point of contact on each side, channel, response/approval timing, escalation contact.
Delayed dependency treatmentWhat happens if access, approvals, data, content, or decisions are late (the clause that protects your timeline).
Change requestsHow out-of-scope work is identified, estimated, approved, and billed.
Deliverable reviewHow deliverables are submitted, reviewed, accepted, or deemed accepted.

Quality bar: no ambiguity about who provides inputs, who does the work, who approves, and how scope changes are handled commercially.

2. Ways of Working and Light Governance Template

Download this section (.md)

Purpose

How the project will be run and how everyone stays aligned — kept proportionate. On a focused build you want enough structure to make decisions and manage risk, not a steering-committee apparatus.

ElementWhat to define
Working team and rolesMerchant business owner, merchant technical/decision contact, partner project lead, partner build/solution lead, and any app/integration contact. One person often wears several hats — name who’s accountable, not a formal org chart.
Decision ownership and escalationWho signs off on scope, design, timeline, and launch; plus a single named escalation contact on each side if a decision stalls.
Risk and issuesA simple shared list: issue, owner, impact, next step, due date. No formal register required.
Change controlHow change requests are raised, priced, approved, and reflected in scope/timeline.
Launch governanceGo/no-go owner, launch-readiness checklist, who’s on call launch day, and how issues are triaged.
Meeting cadence and communicationA regular working check-in, where decisions and notes live (single source of truth), and response-time expectations.
If applicableLight owners for B2B (Plus), POS store operations, or per-market decisions when those are in scope.

Quality bar: a reader can see who owns each decision, how issues get resolved, and how change is controlled — without enterprise overhead.

3. Project Plan Quality Template

Download this section (.md)

Purpose

Turn the SoW into an executable plan: deliverables, dates, owners, dependencies, and a visible path through build, testing, launch, and post-launch support.

ElementWhat to define
Project deliverablesThe artifacts from the SoW (project plan, solution/scope summary, data migration plan if migrating, test/UAT plan, training, launch plan, post-launch support) with dates and owners.
Milestones and dependenciesKey milestones, task-level dates where useful, predecessor/successor links, approval windows, decision deadlines, and the few activities on the critical path.
OwnersClear accountability for major tasks and decisions across partner, merchant, and any third party.
Integration and data milestonesIntegration build/test windows, environment and test-data readiness, migration rehearsal if migrating, validation, and cutover timing — folded into the plan rather than run as a separate testing track.
Testing, launch, and post-launchUAT timing and remediation window, launch-readiness activities, launch milestone, and post-launch support start/end and handoff.
If applicableB2B, POS, or per-market sequencing when in scope.

Quality bar: a reader can see what’s happening, when, who owns it, what it depends on, and how readiness is tracked through launch.

4. Testing / UAT Plan Quality Template

Download this section (.md)

Purpose

Define how the merchant validates that the build supports their real business before launch. The point isn’t that pages load — it’s that the merchant can run the business on Shopify.

ElementWhat to define
ObjectivesWhat testing must confirm before launch.
Scope and coverageStorefront, catalog, cart and checkout, customer accounts, order operations, SEO/analytics, apps/integrations, and data-migration validation. Add B2B, POS, or international flows where in scope. Also confirm the Shop sales channel: branding, products, and active discounts appear correctly in the Shop app.
RolesWho owns test scenarios, who tests (merchant business and technical testers), who triages defects, who makes the launch call.
Entry criteriaEnvironment and data ready, integrations testable, access provisioned, scenarios written, partner QA done.
ScheduleTest window, tester availability, retest timing, decision date.
Defect handlingSeverity levels, how defects are logged (with evidence), who fixes, and closure rules. Keep it lightweight, but written down.
Exit criteriaWhat “ready to launch” means: critical paths pass, no open blockers, business sign-off.

Quality bar: coverage spans real customer journeys, operational workflows, integrations, and data — not just screen checks.

How to use this toolkit

Keep all five sections, but scale each to the build in front of you. A theme-and-apps project might compress Sections 2–4 into a single page; a Plus B2B build with integrations will use them in full. If a section feels like overhead, simplify it — but don’t drop scope clarity, explicit exclusions, testing, or a launch plan, which protect both you and the merchant at every size.

Quality templates describe the quality bar, not a required layout. Partners may use their own formats.
